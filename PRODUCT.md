# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Atlas is for teachers, professors, academic leaders, course teams, schools, colleges, and universities choosing how AI should participate in learning. Students and individual educators can also request access or a demonstration. The first product scope runs from K-12 through undergraduate courses.

Visitors need to understand the teaching model, see the real product, judge whether Atlas fits a course, and choose a concrete way to begin.

## Product Purpose

Atlas is a teacher-controlled agentic learning management system. Teachers set the terms for a course agent in natural language. Students work with a personal agent inside those terms. Questions, hints, revisions, submissions, and review stay connected so teachers can see the learning journey and decide what happens next.

The public site exists to replace vague AI claims with a clear product argument, real product evidence, and an honest path to a first course or demonstration.

## Brand Personality

Exact, authored, alive.

Atlas should feel considered enough for an academic institution and curious enough to suggest a new model for learning. The voice is direct, literate, calm, and builder-owned. It makes a strong argument without hype and lets real product behavior carry the proof.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS card grids and repeated left-copy, right-screenshot sections.
- The same eyebrow, rule, column, or numbered-step grammar recycled across every page.
- Cyber imagery, purple gradients, glassmorphism, fake dashboards, and other AI-generated visual shorthand.
- Decorative blobs or particles repeated outside the living-field hero.
- Detector, surveillance, or policing aesthetics.
- Invented product interfaces or claims that are not present in Atlas.
- Dense technical architecture, implementation, or unfinished compliance claims on the public site.
- A hobby-project tone, excessive whimsy, or motion without narrative purpose.

## Design Principles

1. **Make the model visible.** Show how teacher choices become student work and how that work becomes evidence.
2. **Give each page its own job and shape.** Shared identity comes from voice, type, palette, and precision, not from one repeated layout template.
3. **Use the product as proof.** Real Atlas surfaces take priority over decorative product mockups.
4. **Keep people in control.** Teachers author the terms and interpret the evidence. Students own the important moves in their work. The agent is the medium.
5. **Earn every extraordinary moment.** The living field, product film, and page interactions must clarify the narrative and remain restrained outside their moment of focus.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All navigation, tabs, disclosures, controls, and forms must work with a keyboard and expose clear semantic state. Maintain visible focus, sufficient contrast, useful labels, validation messages, reduced-motion alternatives, readable line lengths, and layouts that remain coherent from mobile through wide desktop screens.
