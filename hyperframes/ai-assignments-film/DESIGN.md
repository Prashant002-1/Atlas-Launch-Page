# AI Assignments film

## Intent

A launch film for Atlas AI Assignments: the argument that policing AI in
education has failed, and the demonstration of what replaces it. A teacher
speaks one sentence; their agent builds the assignment agent; a student works
in the open; grading returns as evidence plus judgment, verified by the
teacher. The film is silent-first: it lives muted on a website, carries its
argument in typography, and holds its final frame.

## Style prompt

Calm, cinematic, premium. A dark ink field like a lecture hall before the
talk begins. Warm paper surfaces carry the human moments (chat, work,
review). Type does the narrative work; product surfaces are suggested with
restraint, never screenshotted chrome. Long deliberate eases, nothing bouncy,
nothing neon. The one saturated color — pine — is reserved for the living
connection between agents.

## Typography

- Display and body: Geist Sans (weights 300–600), sentence case, tight tracking (-0.03em)
- Micro labels: Geist Mono, uppercase, 11–13px equivalent, letter-spacing 0.14em, used sparingly
- Hero claims: large, few words per line, revealed by word/line with long soft eases

## What NOT to do

- No numbered scene labels ("01 / SCENE"), no dashboard chrome, no fake UI screenshots
- No neon glows, gradient meshes, particle fields, equalizer bars, or emoji
- No bounce/elastic easing, no spin, no flash cuts, no jump cuts between scenes
- No walls of transcript text; chat shows at most three exchanges, large and readable
- Never more than one claim + one support line visible at once
- No red "AI DETECTED" alarm aesthetics; the policing critique stays typographic and dry
