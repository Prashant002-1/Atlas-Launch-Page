How Atlas works

The work happens in the open.

The teacher sets the terms. The student works with the course agent. Atlas keeps the reasoning, the answer, and the teacher’s decision in one learning record.

One assignment, four records

Nothing important disappears at submission.

Before the session

Define how the agent may help.

The teacher sets the voice, sources, challenge level, direct-answer policy, assignment boundaries, and rubric. Students see the rules before they begin.

  • Course materials and approved sources
  • What the agent can and cannot do
  • What the student must show
During the session

Make the important moves.

The agent can ask for evidence, challenge an assumption, or offer the next hint. The student still frames the problem, makes the choice, and writes the final work.

  • Questions remain connected to the assignment
  • Hints follow the teacher’s contract
  • Revisions stay visible
After submission

Open the reasoning behind the answer.

The teacher can read the transcript beside the final work. Atlas points to the passages that support each rubric judgment without hiding the rest of the record.

  • Student and agent turns stay distinct
  • Relevant passages are highlighted
  • The full transcript remains available
Teacher review

Judge the learning, then decide what comes next.

Atlas prepares the evidence. The teacher reviews the rubric, changes it when needed, and makes the final decision. Class patterns can become the next lesson or practice set.

  • Teacher judgment stays final
  • Rubric criteria remain inspectable
  • Course planning can use class patterns

Teacher contract

Control stays human

The agent is the medium. The people make the decisions.

Agent may

Explain, question, point to a source, challenge, and offer the next hint.

Student must

Frame the problem, choose the path, make the argument, revise, and submit.

Teacher decides

The terms, the evidence that matters, the grade, and what the class needs next.

The course gives the agent its map. The person gives it continuity.
