How Atlas works

The teacher sets the terms. The student does the work.

Atlas keeps the course rules, the student’s reasoning, the final answer, and the teacher’s review connected.

Scroll to follow one assignment
1 / 4
Teacher

Describe how the agent should teach.

Set the voice, challenge level, use of direct answers, required sources, and boundaries in plain language. The result is readable and specific to the course.

  • Course materials and teaching approach
  • What the agent can and cannot do
  • What students are told before they begin
Student

Work through the problem with the agent.

The student asks questions, tests an idea, uses a hint, and revises. The agent can challenge the reasoning without choosing the answer or writing the submission.

  • The assignment stays in view
  • Hints follow the teacher’s rules
  • The student makes the important moves
Teacher

Read the reasoning behind the answer.

Atlas keeps the transcript and final work together. The teacher can see where the student framed the problem, changed an assumption, and defended the result.

  • Questions and hints remain visible
  • Relevant passages are highlighted
  • The final wording remains the student’s
Teacher

Grade the learning and decide what comes next.

Rubric criteria are tied to the work the student actually did. The teacher reviews the result, changes it when needed, and uses class patterns to plan the next lesson or practice.

  • Teacher judgment stays final
  • Class patterns can shape new practice
  • Each student keeps a personal learning history

Teacher controlled

Change the behavior without changing the course.

Voice and persona
Direct, patient, playful, formal, or written in your own words.
Help level
Questions, hints, examples, direct answers, or none of them.
Sources
Course materials, approved outside research, and required citations.
Assignment rules
What the agent can do for this task and where it must stop.
Student context
Prior work, feedback, recurring mistakes, and current course goals.
Teacher review
Transcript, final work, rubric criteria, and the final decision.
